# GhanaNice visual system

GhanaNice is a people-powered guide to good places across Ghana. It should feel direct, specific and editorial—not like a generic tourism campaign or an AI-generated landing page.

- Voice: plain language, concrete nouns and useful labels. Say “Go for,” “Good to know,” “Most visited,” and “Share a place.” Avoid abstract product language such as “signal,” “flow,” “field” and “journey” when a simpler label exists.
- Typography: oversized Arial/Helvetica display type for confidence, paired with Georgia for an occasional human editorial voice. A section does not automatically get an eyebrow, heading, paragraph and CTA; hierarchy should follow the content.
- Layout: the regional atlas uses tactile postcards rather than a conventional gallery. The visitor ranking uses the supplied Wama reference’s black framing and high-contrast modules, while community contributions use the accumulating wall logic seen in the supplied Wndws reference.
- Colour: bone `#f1eee5` and near-black `#11110f` form the base. Flag-derived red, yellow and green are used deliberately for emphasis; lilac provides one non-literal counterpoint.
- Motion: the opening reel moves vertically like a slot machine, beginning quickly and decelerating into Independence Arch. The second authored motion is the postcard atlas: a passing rail spreads into a field, then collapses into a blurred vertical stack when one region is chosen.
- Regional interaction: selecting a postcard keeps the physical deck visible. Fifteen postcards form a muted vertical index, the selected card stays in colour, and the adjacent reader shows verified regional imagery, a summary, capital and useful facts.
- Data: visitor numbers live in stable modular cards. No flying rows, overlapping text or scroll-gated comprehension.
- Community: the contribution wall is both product proof and invitation. The “nice” control demonstrates lightweight agreement; adding a place is framed as leaving a tip in a conversational sentence, not completing a generic application form.
- Imagery: documentary images must refer to real Ghanaian places or work. Generated media may change atmosphere but must not invent landmarks. Each region carries its own photograph of a named place inside that region — no region borrows another region's scenery. Captions and alt text name what is actually in frame (“Akosombo Dam”, not “Eastern landscape or landmark”), and a region with only one verified photograph shows one image rather than repeating it as a fake second view. Photographers are credited per image in `dist/assets/CREDITS.md`.
- Accessibility: semantic headings, visible focus, keyboard-operable controls, reduced-motion behavior, useful alt text, strong contrast and legible mobile layouts.
