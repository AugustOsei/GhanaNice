# GhanaNice visual system

The site is an editorial field guide, not a tourism template. Its visual language is built from documentary photography, contact sheets, location ledgers, survey coordinates, and the sharp red/yellow/green rhythm of Ghana's flag on a charcoal-and-bone base.

- Typography: compressed, oversized sans-serif display type interrupted by a restrained italic serif voice.
- Layout: asymmetric editorial grids, full-bleed media, film-strip framing, abrupt changes in scale, and visible indexing.
- Motion: transform and opacity for interface choreography; the eight-beat arrival reel is clipped inside the Ghana mask from its first frame, the real atmospheric hero film takes over at rest, and a pinned postcard rail settles from motion into order.
- Regional interaction: perforated 2:3 postcards behave like physical objects. Selection moves the actual cards into a visible deck with the chosen card on top while a compact region page opens alongside it.
- Data storytelling: the final section uses official 2024 attraction figures as a proportional motion field. It borrows the structural rhythm of experimental 3D-stack and sticky-grid studies without copying their styling.
- Imagery: every documentary image must be traceable to a real Ghanaian place or maker. Generated work may change atmosphere but must not invent landmarks.
- Accessibility: semantic navigation, keyboard-operable map paths, strong contrast, reduced-motion mode, video poster fallback, and data-saver fallback.
