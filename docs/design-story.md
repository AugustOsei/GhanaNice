# GhanaNice design story

The site makes a very simple claim: Ghana is nice, and the evidence is distributed across the whole country. The page begins with a fast vertical reel of places. Like a slot machine, it moves quickly, slows visibly and lands on Independence Arch. The Ghana silhouette then expands to reveal the whole image and the brand’s plainspoken promise: “Ghana is nice.”

The sixteen regions arrive as a moving line of postcards and spread into a field. Choosing one does not make the deck disappear: the unselected cards form a blurred vertical index while the chosen postcard remains in colour beside a focused regional page. The page gives a summary, verified imagery, the regional capital, reasons to go and a useful fact.

The visitor ranking provides national context without making the reader chase moving text. Its modular card system borrows the clarity and strong black-and-white framing of the supplied Wama reference.

The final act changes GhanaNice from a presentation into a platform. A growing wall of recommendations borrows the open contribution logic of the supplied Wndws reference: filled tiles sit beside invitations to participate. People can mark a place as nice or leave GhanaNice a tip by completing a natural sentence with the place and location. In this static prototype, those actions stay in the browser; a later backend can research submissions, classify them by region and publish reviewed entries.

The experience moves from spectacle to play, evidence and participation. Ghana itself—not interface jargon or numbered landing-page sections—is the story.
